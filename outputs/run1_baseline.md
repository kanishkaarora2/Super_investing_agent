Sarvottam Cables Ltd (NSE: SRVCABLE) Research Brief

Assumed Current Date: 23 September 2026

1.Snapshot

Sarvottam Cables Ltd is a manufacturer of power cables, wires, and conductors [S3]. For the quarter ended 30 June 2026 (Q1 FY27), the company reported revenue from operations of ₹1,428 crore, marking a 35% increase from the year-ago period [S1]. However, a company press release stated Q1 FY27 revenue as ₹1,248 crore, an 18% year-on-year increase [S3]. Net profit rose approximately 6% to ₹82 crore [S1, S3]. Operating margins contracted by 170 basis points to 11.2%, primarily due to a nearly 14% rise in copper prices during the quarter [S1, S3].

2. Bull Case

Strong Demand and Order Book Growth: The company reports strong demand from power transmission and data-centre projects [S3]. Its order book grew to ₹3,900 crore as of 30 June 2026, up from ₹3,150 crore a year prior [S3]. Management maintains a full-year FY27 revenue growth guidance of 15% to 17% [S2].
Capacity Expansion and Low Leverage:A new plant in Bharuch, Gujarat, is expected to add about 30% to cable capacity and is on track to begin commercial production in Q3 FY27 [S3]. Approximately 70% of the ₹620 crore capex for this plant has already been spent, primarily funded through internal accruals and some term debt, resulting in a low net debt to equity ratio of 0.35 times [S2].
Growing Strategic Segments: Data-centre cables currently represent about 9% of domestic revenue and management expects this segment to double in two years [S2]. Exports also increased their contribution to revenue to 21% in Q1 FY27, up from 16% in Q1 FY26 [S3].
Cost Pass-Through Mechanism: Management expects to pass on higher copper costs to customers over the next one to two quarters, as about two-thirds of its contracts include price-variation clauses [S2, S3].
Decreased Promoter Pledge:The percentage of promoter holding pledged with lenders significantly reduced to 4.1% as of June 2026, down from 35% in March 2024 and 11.5% in June 2025 [S8, S4].
Rising Institutional Investment:Foreign institutional investors (FII) and domestic institutional investors (DII) increased their holdings as of June 2026 compared to prior quarters [S8].

3.Bear Case

Margin Pressure from Raw Materials:Operating margins contracted by 170 basis points in Q1 FY27, reaching 11.2%, primarily due to a significant rise in copper prices [S1, S3]. While cost pass-through is expected, there is a lag of one to two quarters, indicating potential continued short-term margin pressure [S2, S3].
Increasing Receivable Days:Receivable days increased from 78 to 96 over the past year, mainly due to slower payments from two large state utility customers [S2]. This could impact working capital and cash flow, although the company has not reported any defaults [S2].
GST Demand Order:The company received a GST demand order for ₹46.3 crore (including interest and penalty) related to input tax credit for FY2020-21 and FY2021-22 [S7]. While Sarvottam Cables Ltd intends to appeal and expects no material impact, this represents a contingent liability [S7].

4.Open Questions

Q1 FY27 Revenue Discrepancy:Clarification is needed regarding the reported Q1 FY27 revenue, which is cited as ₹1,428 crore by a news article [S1] and ₹1,248 crore by the company's press release [S3].
Long-Term Receivable Management:What specific measures is the company implementing to manage and reduce the increasing receivable days from state utility customers [S2]?
Detailed Cost Impact:A more granular breakdown of the impact of copper price fluctuations on margins, beyond "mostly copper," would help in assessing the effectiveness of price-variation clauses and future margin stability [S2].
GST Demand Resolution:Further information on the expected timeline and potential outcomes of the appeal against the ₹46.3 crore GST demand order would be beneficial [S7].
Potential Name Confusion:A local news report mentions "Sarvottam Cable Network" being penalized in Nagpur for illegal overhead wiring [S6]. Given the differing business descriptions (local cable TV/broadband vs. power cables/wires/conductors), it is likely an unrelated entity, but this potential confusion could be clarified.
